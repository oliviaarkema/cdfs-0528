Four Generations of Michigan Farming

Rooted in West Michigan Since 1907

Country Dairy's story began in the 1880s when Andrew Van Gunst immigrated from the Netherlands to West Michigan as a young boy. Through hard work, perseverance, and a deep faith in this land, Andrew and his wife Jane purchased 80 acres in New Era, Michigan in 1907, starting the farm that would become Country Dairy.

Through generations of early mornings, hard seasons, and a stubborn love for the life of farming, that original 80 acres has grown into something much bigger: a Michigan Centennial Farm, a working dairy of over 1,200 Holstein cows, and one of West Michigan's most beloved family destinations.

Today, the fourth generation of the Van Gunst family carries that same spirit forward, not just as farmers but as hosts. Every farm tour, every scoop of ice cream, and every meal served at our Farm Kitchen is an invitation to share in something that has taken more than a century to build.

A Century in the Making

Country Dairy History

From one immigrant family's dream to a Michigan legacy.

1880s
The Beginning

Andrew Van Gunst immigrates from the Netherlands to West Michigan as a young boy, beginning the family story that would eventually become Country Dairy.

1907
The Land

Andrew and Jane Van Gunst purchase the original 80-acre farm in New Era, Michigan, where they raise dairy cows, cherries, asparagus, and potatoes while building a life for their growing family.

1936–1956
Second Generation

Son Henry and his wife Ellen carry on the tradition, raising seven children on the farm before Henry officially takes over ownership. The roots run deeper.

1964–1968
Third Generation

Wendell Van Gunst returns home after studying dairy science at Michigan State University and serving in the Army. He focuses the farm entirely on Holstein dairy cattle and grows the herd.

1983
Processing Plant Opens

Country Dairy opens its on-farm dairy processing plant, allowing milk from their own herd to be pasteurized, bottled, and distributed fresh throughout West Michigan.

2004
The Farm Store Opens

The Country Dairy Farm Store welcomes its first guests, bringing homemade ice cream, farm-fresh food, and family experiences to the public for the first time.

Today
Fourth Generation & Growing

Now in its fourth generation of family ownership, Country Dairy milks approximately 1,200 cows and welcomes tens of thousands of visitors each year. Same land, same family.
